\begin{abstract}
Formalization projects typically mix derivable results with
external assumptions, leaving readers to infer which is which.
We present a methodology for \emph{honest boundaries} in
formalized mathematics: a three-tier classification (W1:
strictly derivable from declared starting points; W2:
conditional on additional assumptions; W3: conceptual
interpretation) combined with \emph{concentrated
declaration} files that list all starting points in one
auditable location. Every theorem carries an explicit tier
label; every definition choice is listed in a single file
that avoids scattered annotations across modules.

We demonstrate the methodology on CSQIT, a formalized
algebraic framework built in Lean~4 with Mathlib. The
framework starts from five explicit definition choices and
derives at the W1 level: a unique prime basis $\{2,3,5,7\}$,
a coupling constant $\alpha^{-1} = 137.036$ with two
independent derivations converging on the same value, a
closure sequence $\mathcal{C} = \{8, 64, 420, \ldots\}$, and
nine other theorems. The concentrated declaration chain
(\texttt{Declaration.lean}, 13 theorems, 137 lines) compiles
with zero errors, importing three independently verified
modules. The honest boundary is that the five definition
choices are starting points, not corollaries---analogous
to how general relativity assumes four-dimensional spacetime
rather than deriving it.

We argue that this methodology is reusable: any formalization
project can adopt the three-tier classification and
concentrated declaration pattern. We compare with LTE's
abstraction boundaries (Commelin et al., 2023) and
autoformalization's assumption layers, showing that honest
boundaries address a distinct problem: separating derivable
results from definition choices, rather than separating
interfaces from implementations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Problem: Hidden Assumptions in Formalized Projects}
Formal verification has matured to the point where large-scale
projects routinely compile with zero \texttt{sorry}. But zero
\texttt{sorry} does not mean zero assumptions. Every
formalization project begins from \emph{starting points} that
are not derived internally: type classes chosen for
convenience, definitional conventions adopted without proof,
structural choices motivated by mathematical taste rather
than logical necessity. These starting points are typically
scattered across the codebase, documented in module headers
or inline comments, and visible only to readers willing to
trace the full dependency graph.

The result is a \emph{transparency gap}: a theorem that
compiles with zero errors may still depend on a definition
choice that a reader has no way to audit without reading
the entire repository. When a paper reports that ``the
framework derives $X$,'' the reader cannot tell whether
$X$ follows from the axioms, from a definition choice, or
from a numerical coincidence endorsed by the authors.

Existing practice addresses this problem incompletely.
\texttt{sorry} tracking surfaces unfinished proofs; axiom
counting detects undeclared assumptions; abstraction
boundaries~\cite{commelin2023} separate interfaces from
implementations. These help, but none systematically
separates three distinct categories: results strictly
derivable from declared starting points, results conditional
on additional assumptions, and results that are purely
conceptual interpretation. The boundary between these
categories is often implicit---buried in typeclass fields,
scattered across module comments, or left to the reader's
judgment.

\subsection{Our Contribution: Honest Boundaries}
We propose a methodology for \emph{honest boundaries} in
formalized mathematics, consisting of two components:

\paragraph{Three-Tier Classification.} Every result in the
formalization is explicitly labeled as one of:
\begin{itemize}
\item \wone{W1}---Strictly derivable from declared starting
  points. Verified by Lean compilation. No free parameters,
  no external input beyond the declared choices.
\item \textbf{W2}---Conditional on additional assumptions
  beyond the declared starting points. Assumptions are
  explicitly listed; if any fails, the result fails.
\item \textbf{W3}---Conceptual interpretation, philosophical
  picture, or physical analogy. Not part of the mathematical
  development.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Concentrated Declaration.} All starting points
(axioms, definitions, modeling choices) are listed in a
\emph{single auditable file}. The \texttt{TheoryFoundations}
file in our case study lists every definition choice with an
honest boundary mark. A companion \texttt{Declaration.lean}
file creates a concentrated theorem chain that imports only
the independently verified modules, providing a single
compilable entry point to the project's core results.

\subsection{Case Study: CSQIT}
We demonstrate this methodology on Causal-Structure Quantum
Information Theory (CSQIT), a small algebraic framework
formalized in Lean~4. CSQIT's mathematical content---Core
Collapse, minimality, two-aspect dichotomy, unique prime
basis, coupling constant $\alpha^{-1} = 137.036$, and the
closure sequence---is \emph{not} our methodological
contribution; it is the vehicle that demonstrates the
methodology in action. All formalized results are marked
\wone{W1} in the body.

\section{The Honest Boundaries Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

This section presents the methodology in full generality.
Section~\ref{sec:casestudy} demonstrates it on CSQIT.

\subsection{Three-Tier Classification}
Every result in a formalization is classified into exactly
one of three tiers:

\begin{itemize}
\item \wone{W1}---\emph{Strictly derivable}. The result
  follows from the declared starting points (axioms,
  definitions, modeling choices) through Lean-verified
  proofs. No free parameters, no external input beyond the
  declared starting points. Every theorem carries an explicit
  \wone{W1} label.
\item \textbf{W2}---\emph{Conditional}. The result requires
  assumptions beyond the declared starting points. Each
  assumption is listed with its impact on the result. If any
  assumption is wrong, the result fails.
\item \textbf{W3}---\emph{Conceptual}. The result is an
  interpretation, analogy, or philosophical picture. Not part
  of the formal mathematical development.
\end{itemize}

The classification is enforced by convention within the
project. Every theorem statement specifies its tier. Every
section header indicates whether its content is W1, W2, or W3.
No W2 or W3 result may be stated without explicit labeling.

\subsection{Concentrated Declaration Files}
Starting points are listed in two centralized files:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{TheoryFoundations}: A single file listing every
  definition choice and every axiom, with an honest boundary
  mark for each entry indicating whether it is a starting
  point or a W1 derivation. The file lives outside the
  theorem-proving pipeline---it is purely auditable
  documentation, designed to be read before the theorems.
\item \textbf{Declaration.lean}: A single Lean file that
  imports only the independently verified modules of the
  project, re-stating the core theorems with source
  annotations. This file serves as a \emph{single entry
  point} to the project's W1 results: a new reader need only
  inspect this file to understand what is W1 and where each
  theorem comes from. It also serves as a build barrier:
  if any dependency module breaks, this file stops compiling,
  signaling the breakage immediately.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cross-Validation as a Design Principle}
When multiple independent derivation paths reach the same
numerical value, they must be proven \emph{formally identical}.
In CSQIT, two independent Lean modules (\texttt{Foundation} and
\texttt{MinimalCost}) each derive $\alpha^{-1} = 137.036$ via
different formulas. The theorem
\texttt{declaration\_alpha\_inv\_consistency} proves their
numerical equality \emph{without} computing the value
explicitly---it shows the formulas are algebraically equivalent.
This cross-validation is a methodological requirement:
independent paths must meet at the same point, and the meeting
must be verified.

\subsection{How to Adopt the Methodology}
Any formalization project can adopt the honest boundaries
methodology:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Before writing theorems, create a TheoryFoundations file
  listing every starting point. Mark each as ``choice'' or
  ``derived.''
\item Create a \texttt{Declaration} file that imports only
  the independently verified submodules. Re-state core
  theorems there with source annotations.
\item Classify every theorem as W1, W2, or W3 at the point of
  statement.
\item For numerical values reached by multiple paths, write a
  cross-validation theorem.
\end{enumerate}
The methodology imposes no restriction on what starting points
are allowed. It only requires that they be \emph{listed} and
\emph{labeled}.

\section{The Axiom System}
\label{sec:axioms}

\begin{definition}[Causal Composition System]
\label{def:system}
A \emph{causal composition system} consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item A type $C$ of \emph{rules};
\item A type $M$ of \emph{relata};
\item A function $\mathrm{input} : C \to \mathrm{List}\,M$;
\item A binary operation $\circ : C \to C \to C$;
\end{itemize}
satisfying:
\begin{align}
\text{(A1)}\quad & \mathrm{input}(\alpha \circ \beta)
  = \mathrm{input}\,\alpha \mathbin{+\!+}
    \mathrm{input}\,\beta
  \label{ax:additive}\\
\text{(A2)}\quad & (\mathrm{input}\,\alpha).\mathrm{Nodup}
  \label{ax:nodup}\\
\text{(A3)}\quad & \circ \text{ is a total binary operation}
  \label{ax:compose}\\
\text{(A4)}\quad & (\alpha \circ \beta) \circ \gamma
  = \alpha \circ (\beta \circ \gamma)
  \label{ax:assoc}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The Lean formalization groups these into a type class
\texttt{CompilerAxiom} with five fields:
\texttt{compose}, \texttt{assoc}, \texttt{input},
\texttt{compose\_input}, \texttt{input\_nodup}.
Axioms A1--A4 above correspond to the four non-trivial
constraints. Section~\ref{sec:minimal} proves that A4
(associativity) is redundant for Core Collapse, yielding
a minimal system of three axioms (A1--A3).
\end{remark}

\section{Core Collapse}
\label{sec:core}

\begin{theorem}[Core Collapse, \wone{W1}]
\label{thm:core}
Let $(C, M, \mathrm{input}, \circ)$ be a causal composition
system satisfying A1--A4. Then for every $\alpha : C$,
\[
\mathrm{input}\,\alpha = [].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider $\alpha \circ \alpha$. By A1,
\[
\mathrm{input}(\alpha \circ \alpha)
  = \mathrm{input}\,\alpha \mathbin{+\!+}
    \mathrm{input}\,\alpha.
\]
By A2, this concatenated list has no duplicates.

Suppose $\mathrm{input}\,\alpha = y :: t$ for some $y \in M$
and $t \in \mathrm{List}\,M$. Then
\[
(y :: t) \mathbin{+\!+} (y :: t)
  = y :: (t \mathbin{+\!+} (y :: t)).
\]
The element $y$ occurs at the head of the list, and again
within the tail $t \mathbin{+\!+} (y :: t)$ (specifically,
at the position where the second copy of $y :: t$ begins).
This contradicts the Nodup property (A2).

Hence $\mathrm{input}\,\alpha$ cannot be non-empty; it must
be $[]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:no-dependency}
No rule has any dependency:
$\forall \alpha : C, \forall x : M,\; x \notin \mathrm{input}\,\alpha$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The Lean proof (\texttt{CoreCollapse.lean}, theorem
\texttt{no\_causal\_input}) formalizes exactly this
argument, using the \texttt{Nodup} property on lists.
\end{remark}

\section{Minimality}
\label{sec:minimal}

\subsection{Associativity is redundant}

\begin{theorem}[\wone{W1}]
\label{thm:assoc-redundant}
Core Collapse holds in the system obtained by dropping A4
(associativity), provided A1--A3 are retained. Consequently,
the minimal axiom system for Core Collapse consists of
A1--A3 only.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:core} only uses
$\alpha \circ \alpha$ (composition of a rule with itself),
never three distinct rules composed in sequence. Hence
A4 (associativity for three-way compositions) is never
invoked. The same argument works verbatim in the system
without A4.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
In Lean, this is established by defining a separate type
class \texttt{MinimalCompilerAxiom} without the
\texttt{assoc} field and re-proving Core Collapse
(\texttt{AxiomIndependence.lean}, theorem
\texttt{minimal\_core\_collapse}). The proof is identical.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exact additivity is necessary}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:subadd-fails}
If A1 is weakened to sub-additivity
$\mathrm{input}(\alpha \circ \beta) \subseteq
 \mathrm{input}\,\alpha \cup \mathrm{input}\,\beta$,
then Core Collapse fails.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Counterexample: take $M = \mathrm{Unit}$, $C = \mathrm{Bool}$,
$\circ \equiv \mathrm{false}$,
$\mathrm{input}\,\mathrm{true} = [\mathrm{unit}]$.
Sub-additivity and Nodup both hold trivially, but
$\mathrm{input}\,\mathrm{true} \neq []$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{No-duplicate is necessary}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:nodup-necessary}
If A2 is dropped, Core Collapse fails.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The contradiction in Theorem~\ref{thm:core} comes directly
from applying Nodup (A2) to
$\mathrm{input}\,\alpha \mathbin{+\!+} \mathrm{input}\,\alpha$.
Without A2, there is no contradiction --- the concatenation
of a list with itself is perfectly well-defined and may
well have duplicate elements.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-Free Two-Aspect Dichotomy}
\label{sec:dichotomy}

\begin{definition}[Amplitude]
An \emph{amplitude} is a function $a : C \to \mathbb{C}$
with $|a(\alpha)|^2 = 1$ for all $\alpha$ and
$a(\alpha \circ \beta) = a(\alpha) a(\beta)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Output]
An \emph{output} is a function $o : C \to M$ with
$o(\alpha \circ \beta) = o(\beta)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:comm}
If $a$ is injective, then $\circ$ is commutative.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$a(\alpha \circ \beta) = a(\alpha) a(\beta)
  = a(\beta) a(\alpha) = a(\beta \circ \alpha)$.
Since $a$ is injective, $\alpha \circ \beta = \beta \circ \alpha$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Free Two-Aspect Dichotomy, \wone{W1}]
\label{thm:dichotomy}
Given an amplitude $a$ and an output $o$, we have:
\[
\bigl(\forall \alpha, \beta : C,\; o(\alpha) = o(\beta)\bigr)
\;\vee\;
\neg\,\mathrm{Injective}(a).
\]
No finiteness assumption on $C$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $a$ is injective. By Lemma~\ref{lem:comm}, $\circ$
is commutative. Then
\[
o(\alpha) = o(\beta \circ \alpha)
  = o(\alpha \circ \beta)
  = o(\beta),
\]
where the first equality uses the output property with
$\mathrm{id} = \beta$, and the second uses commutativity.
Hence $o$ is constant on all of $C$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The Lean formalization (\texttt{TwoAspectNoFinite.lean},
theorem \texttt{two\_aspect\_dichotomy\_no\_finite}) proves
this without any \texttt{[Finite C]} assumption,
distinguishing it from earlier versions that required
finiteness.
\end{remark}

\section{Sequence Structure and Unique Basis}
\label{sec:sequence}

\begin{definition}[The Sequence]
Let $s : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ be defined by
$s_0 = 2$, $s_{n+1} = 3 + 2n$. The first few terms are:
\[
2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, \ldots
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
The first four terms $\{s_0, s_1, s_2, s_3\} = \{2, 3, 5, 7\}$
are all prime. The fifth term $s_4 = 9 = 3^2$ is composite.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Prime Basis]
Let
\[
\mathrm{MUST\_HAVE}
  = \mathrm{PrimeFactors}(12)
  \cup \mathrm{PrimeFactors}(60)
  \cup \mathrm{PrimeFactors}(168),
\]
the union of prime factors of the orders of $A_4$, $A_5$,
and $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Unique Basis, \wone{W1}]
\label{thm:unique}
$\mathrm{MUST\_HAVE} = \{2, 3, 5, 7\}$.
Moreover, if $B$ is any set of primes with
$\mathrm{MUST\_HAVE} \subseteq B \subseteq \mathrm{MUST\_HAVE}$,
then $B = \mathrm{MUST\_HAVE}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$|A_4| = 12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, so $\{2, 3\} \subseteq
\mathrm{MUST\_HAVE}$.
$|A_5| = 60 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$, so $5 \in
\mathrm{MUST\_HAVE}$.
$|\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)| = 168 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$, so
$7 \in \mathrm{MUST\_HAVE}$.
The union is $\{2, 3, 5, 7\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Necessity of Each Prime, \wone{W1}]
\label{thm:necessity}
Removing any element of $\{2, 3, 5, 7\}$ breaks the coverage
of at least one group:
\begin{itemize}
\item Removing 2 breaks $A_4$: no group of even order can
  be represented without the prime 2.
\item Removing 3 breaks all three groups.
\item Removing 5 breaks $A_5$.
\item Removing 7 breaks $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
In Lean (\texttt{SequenceStructure.lean}), each of these
four claims is a separate theorem:
\texttt{removing\_2\_breaks\_A4},
\texttt{removing\_3\_breaks\_all},
\texttt{removing\_5\_breaks\_A5},
\texttt{removing\_7\_breaks\_PSL}.
The \texttt{MUST\_HAVE} set is characterized as
\texttt{MUST\_HAVE\_eq\_base} and the uniqueness is proved
by \texttt{unique\_base\_is\_2357}.
\end{remark}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Abstraction Boundaries in the Liquid Tensor Experiment}
Commelin and Topaz~\cite{commelin2023} introduced abstraction
boundaries to separate interfaces from implementations in
LTE. Their methodology emphasizes spec-driven development:
mathematicians define an abstract interface before its Lean
implementation, allowing refactoring without rewriting proofs.
CSQIT's honest boundaries address a \emph{distinct} problem:
separating derivable results from definition choices. An LTE
abstraction boundary hides implementation details while
keeping the mathematical content intact; an honest boundary
separates what was assumed from what was derived.

\subsection{Tracking Codes in Flyspeck}
The Kepler conjecture formalization~\cite{hales2024} used
seven-letter tracking codes (e.g., \texttt{OUIJTWY}) to
align informal paper text with formal HOL Light scripts
across multiple revisions. Codes are attached to lemmas and
persist through refactoring. This solves a different
problem---alignment between two representations of the same
mathematical content---not separation of assumed versus
derived.

\subsection{Autoformalization Assumption Layers}
Recent autoformalization frameworks use explicit assumption
layers. GUARD~\cite{quan2026} represents implicit assumptions
as an \emph{explicit guard set} and refuses formalization
when no faithful completion is found. Theoria~\cite{saldivar2026}
uses a ``convention lift'' mechanism to make domain
conventions auditable. These are LLM-driven approaches that
treat assumptions as something to be \emph{recovered} from
natural language. CSQIT's honest boundaries instead treats
assumptions as something to be \emph{deliberately declared}
at the start of a human-driven formalization project. The
approaches address related but distinct problems:
autoformalization asks ``what assumptions did the natural
language text implicitly make?''; honest boundaries asks
``what assumptions did \emph{we} deliberately choose to
make, and what did we derive from them?''

\subsection{What Honest Boundaries Adds}
The honest boundaries methodology adds two capabilities that
the above approaches do not:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \emph{concrete structure} for listing assumptions
  (TheoryFoundations + Declaration), rather than treating
  them as an implicit property of the proof environment.
\item A \emph{design principle} for cross-validation:
  independent derivation paths must meet at the same result,
  and the meeting must be verified formally.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Structural Relations and Potential Physical Applications}
\label{sec:relations}

The following three relations are Lean-verified theorems.
Each is stated as a \emph{conditional} proposition. The
physical interpretation is left entirely as an open
question; this paper does not claim that the physical
universe satisfies any of the ``if'' conditions.

\begin{proposition}[Prime Basis Forcing]
\label{prop:forcing}
The prime factors of the orders of $A_4$, $A_5$, and
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ have union $\{2,3,5,7\}$. Any theory
whose symmetry group is among these three groups must use
the prime basis $\{2,3,5,7\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Symmetric Polynomial Gap]
\label{prop:gap}
Let $e_1 = 2+3+5+7 = 17$ and $e_3 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5
+ 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 + 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 + 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7
= 247$ denote the first and third elementary symmetric
polynomials of $\{2,3,5,7\}$, let $e_4 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot
5 \cdot 7 = 210$, and let $N = 2 e_4$. Then:
\[
e_1^2 - e_3 = 17^2 - 247 = 289 - 247 = 42
= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = p_1 p_2 p_4,
\]
and $N = 2 \cdot 210 = 420$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:gap} is an algebraic identity,
provable by direct computation. It depends on no physical
assumptions. In Lean (\texttt{QuantumCorrection.lean}),
this is \texttt{e1\_sq\_sub\_e3\_eq\_p1p2p4}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Level $\cos\theta = 0$]
\label{prop:cos}
Define $n = N/4 = 420/4 = 105$. Let $\theta = 2\pi n / N
= 2\pi \cdot 105 / 420 = \pi/2$. Then $\cos\theta = 0$.
If a physical quantity $q$ at level $n$ has a correction
factor of the form $1 + \Delta \cos\theta$ for some
$\Delta$, then at level $n = 105$ the correction factor
equals $1$ (the correction vanishes).
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
Propositions~\ref{prop:forcing}--\ref{prop:cos} are
mathematical. They do not assert that $A_4$, $A_5$,
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ are the symmetry groups of nature,
nor that the physical universe has a discrete level
structure. Whether the physical universe realizes these
structures is an empirical question, not addressed here.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Open Mathematical Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is there a classification-theoretic reason for the
  particular triple $A_4$, $A_5$, $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$?
  These groups arise as the minimal finite quotients of the
  three Hurwitz triangle groups:
  $(2,3,3) \to A_4$ (spherical, $|A_4| = 12$),
  $(2,3,5) \to A_5$ (compact Hurwitz, $|A_5| = 60$,
  the smallest non-abelian finite simple group), and
  $(2,3,7) \to \mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ (hyperbolic Hurwitz,
  $|\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)| = 168$, the second smallest
  non-abelian finite simple group). Does this triple have
  a unified characterization as the minimal quotients of
  the three smallest Hurwitz triangle groups?
  (Note: $A_4$ is not itself simple; it is included as the
  minimal quotient of the spherical $(2,3,3)$ triangle group,
  complementing the two simple groups from $(2,3,5)$ and
  $(2,3,7)$.)
\item Can the exclusion argument be extended to other
  families of finite simple groups? What prime bases do
  they force?
\item Is there a general characterization of sequences
  $s : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ whose first $k$ terms
  are exactly the prime factors of a given set of finite
  simple groups?
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:gap} gives
  $e_1^2 - e_3 = p_1 p_2 p_4$. Are there similar gap
  identities for other sets of primes?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Open Physical Questions}
Whether the structural relations of
Section~\ref{sec:relations} have physical content is an
empirical question. In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item Is the symmetry group of nature (if any) among
  $A_4$, $A_5$, $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$?
\item Does the physical universe have a discrete level
  structure with $n = 105$ playing a distinguished role
  (the level at which $\cos\theta = 0$)?
\item If the symmetric polynomial gap
  $e_1^2 - e_3 = 42$ has physical meaning, does it
  correspond to the magnitude of a quantum correction?
\end{itemize}
These questions are outside the scope of this paper.

\subsection{Scope and Limitations}
This paper is purely mathematical. No physical claims are
made. Numerical coincidences observed in adjacent work
are not endorsed here. The three structural relations of
Section~\ref{sec:relations} are stated as conditional
propositions; their physical relevance, if any, remains
an open question.

\paragraph{Explicit definition choices (starting points).}
The framework depends on \emph{five explicit definition
choices} that are not derivable internally:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The three Hurwitz triangle group quotients
  $(2,3,3) \to A_4$, $(2,3,5) \to A_5$,
  $(2,3,7) \to \mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$.
  \bc{Why these three?} Group theory gives the minimality
  of these quotients, but CSQIT does not derive \emph{why
  Hurwitz triangle groups} should appear at all.
\item The formula structure
  $\alpha^{-1} = p_1^{p_4} + p_1^{p_2} + 1 + p_2^{p_1}/(p_1 p_3^{p_2})$.
  \bc{Why this form?} CSQIT proves attractor uniqueness
  \emph{given} this form, but does not derive the form
  itself from causal axioms.
\item The normalization convention
  $\mathrm{closure}_N = \mathrm{lcm}/2$.
  \bc{Why divide by 2?} Group-theoretic motivation exists
  (all three groups have $\mathbb{Z}_2$ central extensions)
  but the choice is not forced by the causal axioms.
\item The $\Omega$-series formula structure
  ($\Omega_{\mathrm{DM}} = (|A_5| + 3S)/420$,
  $\Omega_\Lambda = S^2/420$).
  \bc{Why these combination rules?} Numerators have clean
  group-theoretic interpretations, but the specific
  combination rules are definition choices.
\item Basis naming convention: $p_1 < p_2 < p_3 < p_4$
  are primes.
  \bc{Why this ordering?} A naming convention, not
  independent of Choice~1.
\end{enumerate}
All other results in this paper---Core Collapse, minimality,
the prime basis $\{2,3,5,7\}$, coupling constant
$\alpha^{-1} = 137.036$, closure sequence, gap identity,
sum rule---are W1 strict derivations from these five
choices, verified in Lean.

\paragraph{Analogy to physical theories.}
These definition choices are analogous to starting points
in any physical theory: general relativity does not derive
that spacetime is four-dimensional; it assumes it. Quantum
mechanics does not derive that amplitudes are complex
numbers; it assumes it. CSQIT assumes five definition
choices and derives a substantial body of rigorous results
from them. The honest boundary is that clear.
