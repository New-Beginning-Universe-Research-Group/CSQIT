### Core Contribution

This paper presents a methodology for \emph{honest boundaries
in formalized mathematics}: a three-tier classification system
(W1: strictly derivable from declared starting points; W2:
conditional on additional assumptions; W3: conceptual
interpretation) combined with \emph{concentrated declaration
files} that list all starting points and all W1 results in
single auditable locations.

The methodology addresses a gap we observe in large-scale
formalization projects---from the Liquid Tensor Experiment to
Flyspeck to Mathlib---where zero \texttt{sorry} does not mean
zero assumptions, and readers must trace the full dependency
graph to distinguish what was derived from what was assumed.

Our contribution is methodological:

1. **Three-tier classification.** Every result is explicitly
   labeled at the point of statement. The three tiers are not
   a ranking---they are a classification of type: W1 results
   are theorems; W2 results are conditional theorems; W3
   results are narratives.

2. **Concentrated declaration files.** Two files external to
   the theorem-proving pipeline provide a single audit surface:
   \texttt{TheoryFoundations.lean} lists all definition choices
   with honest boundary marks; \texttt{Declaration.lean} chains
   all W1 theorems with source annotations and compiles
   independently of the rest of the project.

3. **Cross-validation as a design principle.** When two
   independent derivation paths reach the same numerical
   value, their equality is itself formalized as a theorem.
   In our case study, the coupling constant $\alpha^{-1} =
   137.036$ is derived by two independent Lean modules, and
   their equality is a machine-checked theorem
   (\texttt{declaration\_alpha\_inv\_consistency}).

### Case Study: CSQIT

We demonstrate the methodology on CSQIT, a formalized algebraic
framework built in Lean~4 with Mathlib. CSQIT starts from five
explicit definition choices and derives at the W1 level: a
unique prime basis, a coupling constant with two independent
derivations converging on the same value, a closure sequence,
and nine other theorems. The concentrated declaration chain
(\texttt{Declaration.lean}, 13 theorems, 137 lines) compiles
with zero errors, importing three independently verified modules.

---

### Fit for ITP

ITP is the natural home for this work. The honest boundaries
methodology is a discipline that any Lean or Coq project can
adopt---it imposes no restriction on proof style, library, or
logic, and requires only that starting points be listed and
results be labeled. The paper directly addresses concerns
expressed in ITP community discussions about auditability of
large-scale formalization projects, and our Related Work
section connects to LTE's abstraction boundaries (Commelin \&
Topaz, 2024) and other ITP-published large-scale formali\-zations.
