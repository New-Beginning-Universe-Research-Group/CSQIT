### Core Contribution

This paper presents a methodology for honest boundaries in
formalized mathematics: a three-tier classification system
(W1: strictly derivable from declared starting points; W2:
conditional on additional assumptions; W3: conceptual
interpretation) combined with concentrated declaration files
that list all starting points and all W1 results in single
auditable locations.

The methodology addresses a gap we observe in large-scale
formalization projects---from the Liquid Tensor Experiment to
Flyspeck to Mathlib---where zero "sorry" does not mean zero
assumptions, and readers must trace the full dependency graph
to distinguish what was derived from what was assumed.

Our contribution is methodological, with direct implications
for the automated reasoning community:

1. Three-tier classification. Every result is explicitly labeled
   at the point of statement. This classification is actionable:
   automated tools could verify that W1 theorems have no external
   dependencies and that W2 theorems carry complete assumption
   tracking.

2. Concentrated declaration files. Two files provide a single
   audit surface: TheoryFoundations.lean lists all definition
   choices; Declaration.lean chains all W1 theorems
   independently. This structure suggests a natural target for
   automated consistency checking.

3. Cross-validation as a design principle. When two independent
   derivation paths reach the same numerical value, their equality
   is formalized as a theorem. This provides a machine-checkable
   invariant for verifying internal consistency across a
   formalization project.

4. Future work: automated checking. The Discussion section
   outlines how the honest boundaries structure could be extended
   with an automated checker that verifies W1/W2/W3 label
   consistency and external dependency tracking. This is directly
   within the scope of automated reasoning tooling.

### Case Study: CSQIT

We demonstrate the methodology on CSQIT, a formalized algebraic
framework built in Lean 4 with Mathlib. CSQIT starts from five
explicit definition choices and derives at the W1 level: a
unique prime basis, a coupling constant with two independent
derivations converging on the same value, a closure sequence,
and nine other theorems. The concentrated declaration chain
(Declaration.lean, 13 theorems, 137 lines) compiles with zero
errors, importing three independently verified modules.
