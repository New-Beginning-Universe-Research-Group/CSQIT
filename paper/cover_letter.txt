### Core Contribution

This paper introduces a methodology for honest boundaries in
formalized mathematics: a three-tier classification system
(W1: strictly derivable from declared starting points; W2:
conditional on additional assumptions; W3: conceptual
interpretation) combined with concentrated declaration files
that list all starting points and all W1 results in single
auditable locations.

The methodology addresses a gap we observe in large-scale
formalization projects---from the Liquid Tensor Experiment to
Flyspeck to Mathlib---where zero `sorry` does not mean zero
assumptions, and readers must trace the full dependency graph
to distinguish what was derived from what was assumed.

Our contribution is methodological, not domain-specific:

1. **Three-tier classification.** Every result is explicitly
   labeled at the point of statement. The classification
   separates three distinct types of claims that are routinely
   mixed in formalization projects.

2. **Concentrated declaration files.** Two files external to
   the theorem-proving pipeline provide a single audit surface:
   \texttt{TheoryFoundations.lean} lists all definition choices
   with honest boundary marks; \texttt{Declaration.lean} chains
   all W1 theorems with source annotations and compiles
   independently of the rest of the project.

The three tiers are not a ranking---they are a classification
   of type: W1 results are theorems; W2 results are conditional
   theorems; W3 results are narratives. The boundary between
   W1 and W2 is sharp: if a result requires any assumption
   beyond the declared starting points, it is W2.

3. **Cross-validation as a design principle.** When independent
   derivation paths reach the same numerical value, their
   equality is itself formalized as a theorem. In our case
   study, two independent Lean modules derive the coupling
   constant $\alpha^{-1} = 137.036$, and their equality is a
   machine-checked theorem
   (\texttt{declaration\_alpha\_inv\_consistency}).

### Case Study: CSQIT

We demonstrate the methodology on CSQIT (Causal-Structure
Quantum Information Theory), a small algebraic framework
formalized in Lean~4 with Mathlib. CSQIT's mathematical
content---Core Collapse, minimality, two-aspect dichotomy,
unique prime basis, coupling constant---is not our
methodological contribution; it is the vehicle that shows
how the methodology works in practice. All formalized results
are marked W1 and chain into \texttt{Declaration.lean}.

---

### Comparison with Related Approaches

The honest boundaries methodology fills a distinct niche:

- **LTE abstraction boundaries** (Commelin et al., 2023)
  separate interfaces from implementations; honest boundaries
  separate derivable results from definition choices. A project
  could adopt both.

- **Flyspeck tracking codes** (Hales et al., 2017) align
  informal text with formal scripts; honest boundaries classify
  the type of a claim (derivable, conditional, interpretive).

- **Autoformalization assumption layers** (GUARD, Theoria, 2026)
  detect hidden assumptions automatically; honest boundaries
  require the human formalizer to declare them deliberately.
  The two approaches are complementary.

### Fit for JFR

The Journal of Formalized Reasoning's emphasis on
"proof techniques and methodologies and their impact on the
formalization process" directly matches our contribution.
The honest boundaries methodology is a discipline that any
formalization project can adopt---it imposes no restriction
on proof style, library, or logic, and requires only that
starting points be listed and results be labeled.
